8. Holding cash 3 motives
1. Transaction motive (交易需求) : Cash required to meet regular payments, such as wages, pay suppliers. The need for cash can be forecast, for example, using cash budget.
2. Precautionary motive (抵抗风险需求) : Cash held to give a cushion against unplanned expenditure, such as maintenance costs. It cannot be forecast with certainty. This amount of cash held depends on several factors, such as risk attitude, experience.
3. Speculative motive (投机需求) : Cash kept available to take advantage of market investment opportunities as they arise. Cannot be forecast. Such as bulk purchase discount offered by suppliers.

18. Sensitivity analysis:
Def: it tests on how NPV will be affected by changes on project key variables, such as sales volume, sales price, discount rate, etc.
Calculation: calculate the relative change in a given variable make NPV = 0; PV of variable/NPV.
Try to find the most sensitive variable and managers can focus attention on it in order to be successful.
缺点：Only 1 variable change at a time, not consider the interaction between factors.
It assume the change will occur throughout the whole life of the project.
Not consider risk as measured by probability can be argued.
Not really a way of consider risk in investment.

23. Cause of capital rationing: 资本限制成因
Hard capital rationing: External reasons for capital rationing can be related to risk and variability of finance.
It may because of industry wide factors limiting funds.
Also, providers of finance may see a company as too risky to invest in, perhaps because it is highly geared or because it has poor record or poor prospects of profitability and liquidity.
Long-term finance for capital investment may have limited availability, because of the poor state of the economy or banking crisis.
Soft capital rationing: Investment capital may be limited internally because a company does not want to borrow.
It unwilling to take on a commitment to increased fixed interest payments.
A company may wish to avoid diluting existing earnings per share or changing existing patterns of ownership and control by issuing new equity.
Given the uncertainty associated with forecasting future cash flows, a company may limit investment funds in order to create an internal market where investment projects compete for finance, with only the best investments projects being granted approval.

27. Discuss using nominal approach and real term approach in calculating NPV
1. Nominal term approach means:
With the nominal approach, both CF and the discount rate incorporate the effects of inflation. The cash flows are all estimated by specific inflation rates then it will give us a nominal cash flow. The cost of capital need to include the general inflation on investor's required rate of return. This approach is more difficult to prepare as the uncertainty surrounding the rate of inflation.
2. Real term approach means:
Exclude the effects of general inflation. Cash flows use real discount rates. The discount rate used here should exclude the effects of inflation. The real discount rate which represent the investors base level of return for risk before inflation is taken into account.
3. The NPV of an investment will not be affected:
Using different approaches will give the same results if tax is ignored. If tax is delayed, it will be difference as the tax effects on capital allowance.

34. Discuss whether changing dividend policy will affect a company's share price (相关无关理论)
1. M&M 无关理论: 完美市场, 股利政策不影响股价, 投资决策是影响股价的根本因素。
In perfect market, the value of share company depends on investment decision, not on dividend policy. Change in dividend policy will not affect its share price or market value. MV will maximized when it invests all positive NPV projects.
2. Relevance theory 相关理论: against M&M, it believe there is a link between dividend policy and share price.
3. Bird in hand argument: as future is highly uncertain, some shareholders prefer to have certain cash dividend now than uncertain capital gain in future.
4. Signaling effect: in real world capital markets are not perfect but semi-strong efficient. Since perfect info is not available, it is possible that the dividend policy will affect the share price. 且股价变动取决于股东预期。
5. Clientele effect 客户效应: Some shareholders may attracted to company by dividend policies. A company with stable dividend is likely to have an established dividend clientele. If change ..., shareholders may sell their shares ... decrease the share price.
However, in perfect market, as there's no transaction cost, changing policy will not change share price.

39. 为什么 SME 融资难?
SME tend to be unquoted and often are owned by a relatively small number of individuals.
The funding gap:
For current investors, they might have already reached the limit to their investment capability, have no more cash available.
For outside investors, it's difficult to raise money from outside as the companies are unquoted. SME are always more risky because its investment from investors is difficult to liquidate.

The business often do not have track record and their accounts will not have been monitored as plc. Becoming quoted is always an expensive process and involves meeting strict requirements. It may also potentially lead to a loss of control of business for existing owners.
For bank funding, they may be not available to meet banks' requirements. They might not have good enough track record to finance.
The maturity gap:
Even if the business can obtain a loan, it may find that the banks are only willing to offer long-term loans not short-term loans because of NCA of the business. For short-term loan, SME always have no assets to match.

46. Explain the term of systematic risk and business risk and financial risk, and how to measure them.
1. Business risk: it refers to the variability in shareholder return due to its business operation. It's always caused by the company's industry because different industry has different capital structure. For example, capital intensive companies will have high level of fixed costs. Business risk can be measured by operating gearing = contribution/PBIT.
2. Financial risk: it refers to the variability in shareholder return due to its need to pay interest. Shareholders may face reduction in dividends as interest must be paid when the company has cash flow shortage. Financial risk arises because of using debt finance, it related to its capital structure. It can be measured by capital gearing = D/E or interest cover = PBIT/interest.
3. System risk: it also called market risk. It refers to the macro-economic factors which affect the cash flows of all companies in the stock market. Because of that, it will remain even if the investors hold perfect portfolio.
If a company has no debt finance, the beta asset is used to measure the business risk.


If a company has debt finance, the beta equity is used to measure the business and financial risk.

47. CAPM assumptions:
1. Perfect portfolios - only consider systematic risk
2. Single-period transaction horizon: To compare the returns on different assets such as shareholdings, the CAPM assumes that all returns are over a standard sing-period transaction horizon, usually 1 year.
3. Perfect capital market - no transaction costs, no tax, all ration, perfect info.
4. Borrowing and lending at the risk-free rate.
